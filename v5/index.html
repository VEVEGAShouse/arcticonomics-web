I — First, what is economics?

The system that decides who lives in a mansion and who ends up homeless.

It uses money to set the value of things and to decide who gets what. And value is never fixed. It depends on three things. Play with them.

Its value

$40

Plenty of it. Some want it.

Infinite and unwanted: free. Limited and needed by everyone: its price rises as high as people can pay. That is World Economics.

II — The law of Arcticonomics

The Arctic runs counter‑cyclical to the world.

Arcticonomics is part of World Economics, but it moves in the opposite direction. Same Arctic. Different world.

World economy

Fragmented

  • Everyone trusts each other
  • Trade flows freely across borders
  • The cheapest, most competitive wins

  • Mistrust replaces trust

  • People pay whatever it takes

  • The last frontier

V — The great Nordic paradox

The Nordic impossible dilemma.

Five Arctic nations. The values they champion most are at odds with where their wealth comes from. Try to have both.

Today: a wealthy Nordic region, in a world at war.

Norway · oil

Peace brings Venezuela, Iran and Russia back to the market. Their cheap oil floods the world and the price collapses.

Sweden · defence

A world that settles conflicts through dialogue does not buy weapons. A cornerstone of Swedish industry disappears.

They cannot compete on price. They win only when they are the exception, not the rule.

The Nordics move forward when the rest of the world moves backward.

VI — The real risk

Not a scandal. A credibility crisis.

Every country protects its own interests. That is normal. The problem is the brand: the Nordics made peace, democracy, freedom, equality and human rights their shared identity, and became the world’s moral superpowers.

VII — Time to rethink the idea of the Nordics

The problem is not economic. It is the story.

The answer is not to bend the economy to fit the story, but to write a new story that fits the economy. Don Quijote refused to accept that the world had moved on, and fought windmills. The mistake is not in dreaming. It is in refusing to let the dream evolve.

  • What does it mean to be Nordic in the 21st century?
  • What is our role in this new world?
  • What new ideas can we genuinely stand for?

Not to give up on the Nordic dream. To have the courage to dream a new one.
